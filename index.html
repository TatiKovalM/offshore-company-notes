The federal layer for US LLCs

Delaware and Wyoming differ in state fees. The IRS rules are the same in both: a single-member LLC with a foreign owner files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, and a late Form 5472 costs $25,000. FinCEN BOI reports no longer apply to companies created in the US after the final rule of August 14, 2026. A full comparison of the two states is in Delaware vs Wyoming.

What the table leaves out

Registry duties are the company's. The owner has a separate set: controlled foreign company reports in the country of residence, and the risk that a company run from home becomes tax resident there. Substance declarations and financial returns are two different filings in most island jurisdictions, and confusing them is the most common reason a deadline slips. The logic behind substance rules is explained in a guide to economic substance, and a side-by-side view of more than twenty jurisdictions is at offshore jurisdictions compared.

How I use this sheet

For every company I track, the calendar gets four lines: financial year end, the registry payment date, the substance or annual report deadline, and the date someone last checked whether the company's activity has changed. A holding company that starts lending to its group, for example, moves from a light substance test to the full one. Figures are taken from the official schedules as described on the linked pages in November 2026; fees change, so check the current schedule before paying.
